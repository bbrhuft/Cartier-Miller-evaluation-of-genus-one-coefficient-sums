## 1. Summary of the verdict

Every one of the eight claims C1–C8 survives independent recomputation. I found no numerical disagreement of any kind between my results and the first worker's: the family constants, the multiplier, the reverse roots, the class polynomial, all thirteen integer invariants with their complete factorizations, the fixture values at p = 73, and the behaviour of the recognizer on every input I could construct all agree. I additionally proved the transport identity β′ = 2β − qH myself (the note's argument is the same and I consider it correct), and I tested it in 2292 random instances at 61 primes including primes ≡ 2 mod 3 and supersingular curves, with no failure. The adversarial campaign against `depth_three_recognize.py` (about 70,000 inputs at eligible primes, exhaustive at p = 73, 97 and 193, plus ineligible primes, composite and malformed moduli, tampered certificates, the retained audit fixtures and four primes up to 63 bits) produced no false acceptance, no missed family member, and no unexpected exception. ## 2. What I computed symbolically (C1, C2, C3, C5 constants, C6)

## 3. What I computed for the Cartier coefficients and the family (C2 transport, C4, C7, C8)

My `hasse_beta_exact` computes H = [x^{p−1}]F^h and β = [x^{p−2}]F^h by the exact integer multinomial sum Σ h!/(i!j!k!) A^j B^k over 3i + j = n, reducing modulo p only at the end; it was checked against a separate dense square-and-multiply of the polynomial mod p in 35 random cases, and H was checked against a Legendre-symbol point count in 150 random nonsingular curves at twenty primes from 7 to 193 with no mismatch. For every A = 0 seed at every tested prime ≡ 1 mod 3 I confirmed β = 0 and H ≠ 0.

The transport test picks random (A, q) at a prime, sets B = −q³ − Aq, applies the quotient, and compares exact (H′, β′) with (H, 2β − qH). It was run 2292 times at the 61 primes 7, 11, …, 293, 1009, 1033 with zero mismatches in either coordinate. Because the identity is the one load-bearing analytic fact in the whole note, I also proved it independently (the note's Section "Normalized data and the transport step" argues the same way and I consider it correct). With u = F′(q) = A + 3q², Vélu's normalized map is X = x + u/(x − q), so φ*(X dX/Y) = (x + u/(x − q)) ω. Writing F = (x − q)g with g = x² + qx + q² + A, the function v = y/(x − q) satisfies 2 dv = (g′ − g/(x − q)) ω, hence C(g ω/(x − q)) = C(g′ ω); since g/(x − q) = x + 2q + u/(x − q) and g′ = 2x + q, this gives u·C(ω/(x − q)) = C((x − q) ω). Therefore C(φ*(X dX/Y)) = C(x ω) + C((x − q) ω) = (2β − qH) ω, and because the Cartier operator commutes with pullback along the separable map φ, this equals φ*(β′ω′) = β′ω. The only inputs are q ∈ F_p (so that q^{1/p} = q), p odd, and the standard functoriality of C.

For C4 I checked that no prime p ≡ 1 mod 24 lies below 73, that at the six primes 13, 37, 61, 109, 157, 181 (≡ 13 mod 24) a brute-force search over all A = 0 seeds and all rational roots finds zero nondual rational chains of length three, and, stronger than the claim, that H₋₁₉₂ has no root at all in F_p for those primes (so no F_p-curve with CM by the order of discriminant −192 exists there, consistent with p ≠ x² + 48y²). Why the B = 0-seed depth-three classes are rejected deserves a sentence, since the task asked for an explanation and the note gives none beyond "rejected by this family". Three nondual 2-isogeny steps from j = 1728 (discriminant −4) descend the 2-volcano to conductor 8 in Z[i], discriminant −256, not −64 (which is depth two). I confirmed that the eight p = 73 targets (16, 27), (2, 59), (6, 6), (48, 51), (53, 34), (17, 37), (51, 5), (42, 18) have j ∈ {7, 27, 64, 66}, precisely the four roots of `polclass(-256)` mod 73, and that none of them is a root of H₋₁₉₂, H₋₆₄ or H₋₄₈. Rejection at every eligible prime, not just 73, follows from the resultant computed in Section 2, or more conceptually from the fact that an ordinary curve's endomorphism algebra is Q(√−1) in one case and Q(√−3) in the other.

Two further independent observations came out of the campaign. ## 5. Literature verification

Lario's table at https://web.mat.upc.edu/joan.carles.lario/ellipticm.htm (opened) is titled "Elliptic curves with CM defined over extensions of type (2,…,2)" and explains its format as "{D,{d_1,…,d_i},P(x)}, where K = Q(sqrt(D)); Q(j) = Q(sqrt(d_1),…,sqrt(d_i)); P(x) = Irr(j,Q;x)". Its h_4 list contains exactly `{-192,{2,3},x^4-8041801037378436000*x^3+15705521635909735050750000*x^2+826335556188178615474500000000*x-1080060886113159937649308593750000}`, which confirms both the quartic and Q(j) = Q(√2, √3); its h_2 list contains `{-48,{3},x^2-2835810000*x+6549518250000}` and `{-64,{2},x^2-82226316240*x-7367066619912}`, matching P₃ and P₂. PARI's `polclass(-192)`, `polclass(-48)`, `polclass(-64)` return the same polynomials, and my own elimination of j from the family reproduces the quartic, so C3 rests on three agreeing sources. I attempted to find a fourth in the LMFDB (curves over the field 4.4.2304.1 = Q(ζ₂₄)⁺ = Q(√2, √3)); the pages I opened, https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/4.4.2304.1/1.1/a/17 and https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/4.4.2304.1/16.1/a/6, are CM curves of discriminants −288 and −96 respectively, and the search URL with a `cm=-192` parameter did not apply the filter, so I could not confirm a −192 entry there and do not claim one.

Sutherland's 18.782 Lecture 25 was opened at both https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.782/2013fa/LectureNotes25.pdf and the OCW address cited by the note, https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-782-introduction-to-arithmetic-geometry-fall-2013/e011c006f6aeb95083197457da90598d_MIT18_782F13_lec25.pdf (the shorter address https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.782/LectureNotes25.pdf returns 404). The section list is 25.1 Overview of Mordell's theorem, 25.2 Elliptic curves with a rational point of order 2, 25.3 2-isogenies, 25.4–25.7 weak Mordell–Weil, heights and the proof. Section 25.3 states for E: y² = x(x² + ax + b) that "the 2-isogeny φ: E → E′ sends O to O′ and each affine point (x, y) on E to (X, Y) = (x + a + b/x, y(1 − b/x²)) on E′" with E′: Y² = X(X² + AX + B), A = −2a, B = a² − 4b, gives the dual, and states [2] = φ̂∘φ. It says nothing about invariant differentials, so the normalization φ*ω′ = ω used by the note is not quoted from Sutherland but follows from the displayed formula (dX/Y = (1 − b/x²)dx/(y(1 − b/x²)) = dx/y), which the note correctly derives itself.

Katz's paper was opened as the author-hosted file https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/zannier22.pdf (the note cites `zannier_publ.pdf`; the two files were not compared) and the publication record at https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/on-a-question-of-zannier/, which gives Experimental Mathematics 30(3), 2021, pp. 422–428, DOI 10.1080/10586458.2018.1551818, resolving the "issue-date metadata" the note left open. Section 1 reads: "We fix an integer N ≥ 1 and an elliptic curve E over Z[1/6N], given by an equation y² = f(x) … On E we have the differential of the first kind ω = dx/y and the differential of the second kind η = xdx/y. For each prime p not dividing 6N, we look at this data mod p, and apply the Cartier operator C_p. We get quantities α_p, β_p ∈ F_p defined by C_p(ω) = α_pω, C_p(η) = β_pω", and identifies α_p as "the coef. of x^(p−1) in f(x)^(p−1)/2 mod p" and β_p as "the coef. of x^(p−2) in f(x)^(p−1)/2 mod p". Theorem 3.1 reads: "For any prime p ≥ 5, α_p is the reduction mod p of E_{p−1}, and β_p is the reduction mod p of E_{p+1}/12. Moreover, α_p and β_p have no common zero." Claim (c) is therefore confirmed verbatim in this version; the word "isogeny" does not occur in the paper, so Katz is a source for the definitions only, not for any transport rule.

On prior work, I searched for an explicit recognition of discriminant −192 CM curves from (A, B) with recovery of the twist parameter, and for the identity β′ = 2β − qH. I found neither. The closest items I actually opened are Charles, "Complex multiplication tests for elliptic curves" (https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0409501, 2004), which tests CM over number fields by supersingular-prime statistics and Galois representations and does not use class polynomials or twist parameters; Zannier's recent arXiv:2512.00505 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00505 and its PDF), which concerns congruences for a specific sequence and mentions the Cartier operator only in passing, with no x^{p−2} coefficient, no isogeny formula and no reference to Katz's paper; and Movasati's "Quasi-modular forms attached to elliptic curves: Hecke operators" (https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2408), whose abstract concerns Hecke operators on geometric quasi-modular forms and which I could not confirm contains a 2-isogeny transformation formula for the E₂-type coordinate because only the abstract was readable. Conceptually the transport identity is the mod-p shadow of the classical transformation of the differential of the second kind under Vélu's normalized isogeny (φ*η′ = 2η − qω modulo exact differentials, which the note itself proves as a rational-function identity) combined with Katz's identification β_p ≡ E_{p+1}/12 and the classical congruence E_{p+1} ≡ E₂ mod p; but I did not open Vélu (1971) or Serre's p-adic modular forms paper and I do not cite them as having the formula. The recognition problem itself is, over F_p, equivalent to testing H₋₁₉₂(j) ≡ 0 mod p and reading off the sextic-twist parameter, which is standard CM-method material (Atkin–Morain and later work, not reopened); the specific relative-norm extraction over the biquadratic algebra, which avoids computing j, square roots and class polynomials, is the only element I could not locate elsewhere, and the note correctly claims no novelty.

## 7. Criticisms of the note and the code

The following list is ordered roughly by importance. Where I quote, the sentence is verbatim from `Depth_Three_Recognition_Proof_Note_20261007.md`.

1. Overstated cost framing. The cost table says "Complete unconditional preparation for an accepted curve | T_Schoof(E,p)+O(1) field operations; the trace is the whole non-constant cost", and the one-paragraph outcome says "The missing coefficient K of the original normalized cubic then follows from a certified exact trace". For an accepted curve the exact trace is not an independent unknown: End(E) = O₋₁₉₂ forces 4p = t² + 192f², whose solution (t₀, f) is unique up to the sign of t₀ and is computable by Cornacchia's algorithm in O(log² p) bit operations without any point counting (verified at six primes in Section 4). The only genuinely missing datum is one sign bit, and a single random point P with [p + 1 ∓ t₀]P decides it whenever the trial is conclusive (it is inconclusive only when P ∈ E(F_p)[2t₀], and a failed trial is detectable, so the procedure is Las Vegas; I have not proved a uniform bound on the failure probability and do not claim one). Schoof's deterministic polynomial-time algorithm is therefore an unnecessarily expensive reference cost for this family, and `prepare_from_trace` should at minimum reject any supplied trace with 4p − t² ∉ 192·(Z_{>0})², which would have caught the impossible traces 11, 0 and the wrong-sign −10 that it currently accepts at p = 73 (Section 4, row G). The note nowhere mentions that the trace is p-determined up to sign, although its own census shows H taking only two values per prime.

2. "Rejected" tampering is narrower than the sentence suggests. "Nine tampered certificates were rejected, as were six invalid inputs" is true for the tamperings tested, but `verify_supplied_certificate` compares only `beta_multiplier` and `forward_roots` with the stored copy and silently discards everything else; a stored certificate with an altered `reverse_path`, `forward_models`, `depth` or `forward_seed` is returned corrected without any signal. This is consistent with the docstring "stored output is untrusted" but the note should say that only the parameters, the multiplier and the forward roots are checked against the stored record.

3. The integer-factorization exclusions are presented as the proof of facts that are theorems of CM theory, without saying so. "Disjointness from every depth-at-most-two class at eligible primes | Exact evaluations and resultants of the class polynomial | Proved by integer factorization" and "These are global factorization statements, not extrapolations from the finite census" are correct, but Deuring's reduction theorem gives End(Ē) ≅ O₋₁₉₂ for ordinary reduction at p ∤ 8, so no curve with j ∈ {0, 54000} or j a root of H₋₄₈, H₋₁₂, H₋₆₄, H₋₁₆, H₋₄ or H₋₂₅₆ can share a j-value with a family member at any p ≡ 1 mod 24, and the separability of H₋₁₉₂ mod p at primes splitting completely in the ring class field (Cox, Primes of the form x² + ny², Theorem 9.2 and the surrounding theory; not reopened by me) gives the four distinct conjugates. The factorizations are a valuable finite confirmation and they are all correct, but the note's remark that "The pattern of residues is itself a consistency check" undersells the situation: the pattern is forced, and saying so would both strengthen the proof and explain why the B = 0 depth-three classes are rejected, which the note asserts without explanation.

4. A factual slip about degenerations. "At p ∈ {23,47,71,167,191}, all congruent to 23 modulo 24, both square roots exist but the seed is supersingular, and for one of the four sign choices γ ≡ 0, so that conjugate collapses to j = 1728". At p = 23 two sign choices have γ ≡ 0 and a third has α ≡ 0 (so one conjugate collapses to j = 0), as the exponents 23² in N(γ) and 23¹ in N(α) predict and as the first worker's own `integer_invariants_depth3.json` records; the sentence is accurate only for 47, 71, 167 and 191.

5. Loose wording that reads as stronger than it is. "a single explicit two-parameter family over the biquadratic field" describes, for each fixed embedding, a one-parameter twist family of one curve; the "two parameters" are a discrete pair of signs and r. "re-derived by hand and by two independent exact-arithmetic scripts" describes two scripts by the same author sharing the same formulas; the present report is the first independent re-derivation. "defined and ordinary over F_p precisely when p ≡ 1 (mod 24)" is correct as a conjunction but could be misread as saying the coefficients are irrational at p ≡ 23 mod 24, where in fact all four conjugate models are F_p-rational and supersingular.

6. The obstruction at p ≡ 13 mod 24 is weaker than what is true. "no rational nondual third step exists at all; this is an exact obstruction, not a search miss" is correct, but at those primes there is no F_p-rational curve with CM by O₋₁₉₂ whatsoever (H₋₁₉₂ has no root mod p because p is not of the form x² + 48y²), which I verified at 13, 37, 61, 109, 157, 181 and which would make the "deferred" question of other depth-three routes to the same j moot at those primes.

7. The deferred B = 0 material is flagged as conjectural when part of it is classical. "The finite observation that the primes 73,89,113,233 are of the form x²+64y² while 97,193,241 are not is recorded as an unverified conjectural identification with the classical splitting condition; no source was checked for it" is honest, but the identification is Cox's Theorem 9.2 with n = 64 (p = x² + 64y² iff H₋₂₅₆ has a root mod p, for p ∤ 2·disc), together with Gauss's criterion that for p ≡ 1 mod 4, 2 is a quartic residue mod p iff p = x² + 64y²; the note could cite it instead of leaving it open. (I did not reopen Cox for this report either, so I record it as a pointer, not a verified quotation.)

8. Two small code-hygiene points. `check_short` accepts Python booleans (`True` is treated as 1); `recognize_depth_three` raises `AssertionError` rather than returning a status if α or γ vanished at an eligible prime, which is unreachable by the proof but means a caller cannot distinguish a proof violation from a programming error without catching the exception. Neither affects correctness on valid input.

9. Source citation details. The Katz citation lists the author-hosted file `zannier_publ.pdf` and says "Final issue-date metadata were not re-resolved"; the metadata are Experimental Mathematics 30(3), 2021, 422–428. The verified quotations in this report come from `zannier22.pdf`; theorem numbering in the published version was not checked by me.

10. One sentence in the completeness argument relies on an unstated relabeling. "the seed must be (0,−r³), the first quotient is forced" ignores that at p ≡ 1 mod 3 the seed x³ − r³ has three rational roots r, ωr, ω²r; the chain started at ωr is the chain started at the seed (0, −(ωr)³) with parameter ωr, so the parametrization by all r ∈ F_p^× absorbs it, but the sentence should say so.

Nothing in the list above changes the mathematical outcome: the family is what the note says it is, the recognizer does what the note says it does, and the proof of the recognition theorem is, after my own re-derivation, correct for every p ≡ 1 mod 24.
