Cartier–Miller/T3 integration experiment, 18 October 2026

The frozen candidate is a standalone boundary-registry adapter to the retained Cartier–Miller rounded-third provider. Input is a prime 11 <= p < 2^63 with p == 5 modulo 6. It returns the complete tuple (B_L,a_L,U_p(L),G), where L=(p+1)/3 and G=((p-2)/3)! modulo p. It also returns the adjacent prefix and sum at m=L-1. Frozen source kernels remain unchanged. No GRH or PARI class-number backend is used.

The identities to check are a_L=(3/2)G^(-3), a_m=-4a_L, B_m=B_L+4a_L, U_p(L)=4B_L-(17/3)G^(-3), U_p(m)=4B_L-(16/3)G^(-3), and U_p(L)-U_p(m)=-(1/3)G^(-3). All are modulo p. The only zero of the rounded-third boundary multiplier in this domain is p=17; full boundary outputs are nevertheless acquired and checked there. These are retained mathematical identities and elementary consequences, not new conjectures.

Auto dispatch uses native FLINT below 10^7. Above that bound it ranks eligible q in [5,89], K >= 10^4, by the source R3.1 frozen predictor against its nodal predictor. It attempts T3 only for predicted improvements, and otherwise or after certified reconstruction misses uses the unconditional FLINT nodal anchor. This is a bounded, portable implementation policy, not a guarantee of fastest execution or a universal crossover. Every call builds a fresh Jacobi context. Exact fixed-conductor plan and policy tables may remain loaded between calls. The retained Miller provider reruns arithmetic; no complete answer is cached.

Development inputs are all primes below 20,000 and explicit pilots 1000151, 10000103, 100000223 and 20000000549. Prior source ledgers, their confirmation primes and benchmark primes are additional exclusions for confirmation and sparse benchmark selection. Internal hash freezes have no external timestamp.

Fresh confirmation selection is deterministic, after this freeze. Classes are A=11 modulo 12, B=5 modulo 24, C=17 modulo 24. In each class choose the first eligible, unexcluded prime at or above 5,000,003 and 70,000,000 with q=11 dividing p-1; at or above 7,000,000,000 with q=23/31/47 for A/B/C respectively; and at or above 700,000,000,000 with q=23/31/47 respectively. Add one prime in each class at or above 7,000,000,000 with no q in [5,89] dividing p-1, and one class-C prime at that bound with q=89. Add one class-C prime at or above 7,000,000,000,000 with q=47 as an exact extension check, outside the benchmark grid. The small tier is checked against the original weighted recurrence, the independent scaled binomial prefix and a direct modular binomial product; exact integer binomials were checked throughout development below 20,000. Every tier compares the complete adapter with native factorial and nodal boundaries and compares its Miller prefix with the independently coded elliptic Miller provider. Forced T3 uses each declared q. Native factorial G checks and all six identities are required.

Single-prime benchmarks use the 70-million, 7-billion and 700-billion tiers plus the three no-small-factor cases, with three rotated cold samples of native FLINT + retained Miller, nodal + retained Miller, and automatic T3 + retained Miller. Prime search and imports are outside timing; actual primality checks, registry/provider preparation, conductor discovery, reconstruction, products, all normalizations and collection are inside. Every method returns the same full tuple. Reference comparisons are outside timed regions. Kernel diagnostics retain prefix and boundary time separately. These benchmarks run without concurrent arithmetic jobs.

Matched dense benchmarks evaluate every eligible prime below 2,000, 20,000 and 100,000 by native-FLINT/Miller, the new automatic adapter, the byte-preserved Python forest and the byte-preserved optional GMP forest. Each receives three rotated samples, charging sieve, arithmetic, collection and canonical tuple hashing, excluding disk I/O. The auto method is expected to choose native at these small primes; this fact must be reported rather than credited as T3 acceleration.

A sparse moderate-prime comparison uses sixteen primes: for j=0..15, take the first unexcluded prime at or above 1,000,000+100,000*j in the cycling classes A/B/C with 5 dividing p-1. Compare native, auto, forced T3(q=5), Python forest and GMP forest on precisely that schedule. The forest pays for recurrence indices from zero, not from the first queried prime. Maximum forest cutoff is capped at 2,000,000. High-prime forest comparisons are marked outside this experiment's resource cap rather than assigned invented timings.

The exploratory survey uses the GMP forest for all eligible primes below 1,000,000 and records exact zeros of U_p(L), zeros of U_p(m) and simple identity residuals. It tests, rather than assumes, the tempting statement U_p(L) != 0. All rows below 20,000 receive definition-level checks; sixty-four rows above that bound, selected with seed 20261018, receive independent complete definition and adapter checks. The survey is newly executed evidence on an already studied range, not disjoint confirmation or a new theorem.

No performance improvement over every existing tool, no uniform exponent improvement and no publication or priority claim is proposed. Report gains, losses, fallbacks, counts of distinct primes, conditionality and all unavailable comparisons explicitly.
