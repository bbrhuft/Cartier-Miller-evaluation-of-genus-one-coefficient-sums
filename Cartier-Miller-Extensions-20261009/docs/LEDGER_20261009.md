## Derivation checkpoint and sign conventions

At the branch Q=(mu,0), take local parameter z=v. Squarefreeness gives w=mu+z^2/f'(mu)+O(z^4). Hence omega=dw/v=(2/f'(mu)+O(z^2))dz and

    theta = dw / ((1-w/mu)v) = -2mu*z^(-2) dz + O(1) dz.

This is a residue-free second-kind pole; the old residues -r,+r and r=mu/v0 are undefined when v0=0. The principal part equals d(2mu/z). This local equality does not prove a global fast evaluation formula: a global exact subtraction and its other poles must be tracked. No limit substitution into the admissible formula is justified here.

For the retained admissible formula, theta has residues -r at Q+ and +r at Q-, while div(F_D)=M(Q+-Q-). Thus M theta+r dlog(F_D) cancels residues. The retained model has omega=-2 dX/(2Y); nonvertical Miller lines contribute positive slope with z=-X/Y, so Psi=(alpha_plus-alpha_minus)/(-2). These conventions constrain any new formula but are not themselves a new proof audit.

Katz's standard cubic basis uses omega0=dX/Y and eta=X dX/Y with C(eta)=beta omega0, beta=[X^(p-2)]F(X)^((p-1)/2). His published paper was retrieved from the author's Princeton page and Section 1 inspected. This verifies the additional coefficient framework, not a fast algorithm or novelty. The scale relative to dw/v and the new second-kind representative must be derived explicitly; beta is not automatically supplied by a point count.

Source: Katz, N. M., On a Question of Zannier, published author-hosted PDF, https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/zannier_publ.pdf, DOI 10.1080/10586458.2018.1551818. Accessed 6 October 2026. Only the stated Section 1 coefficient facts are used at this stage. Broader citation tracing remains pending. A guessed Voloch PDF URL failed retrieval and supplies no evidence.

## Branch-point investigation: completed bounded stage, 6 October 2026

Question and assumptions: p≥7 prime, squarefree cubic f=1+cw+bw²+aw³ over F_p with a≠0, and a supplied nonzero rational root μ. Evaluate the fixed-cutoff T_f(μ)=S_f(1/μ), with differential and coefficient conventions explicit and preparation separate from query work.

## 9 October 2026: rounded supersingular third point

Question: for p ≡ 2 (mod 3), can the boundary a_L at L=(p+1)/3 be recovered cheaply, so that the retained O(log p) prefix B_L yields the complete original weighted sum U_p(L)? Also, does CM trace data suffice? This continues the roadmap's second direction and the bridge (B6) recorded above. Work and evidence are in research/rounded_third_boundary_20261009/.

Before this work, verify_handover.py passed in a fresh extraction with 321 hashed files. A separate brute-force check of rooted transport (A′=−4A−15q², B′=−8Aq−22q³, H′=H, β′=2β−qH) agreed on 432 random curves for 11 ≤ p < 400. The depth-one relation β=−rH for the A=0 seed family agreed at 36 primes p ≡ 1 (mod 3). Both checks are fresh standard-library code and are not proof review.

Endpoint: with m=(p−2)/3=⌊p/3⌋ and L=m+1, every exponent 3i ≤ p−1 has i ≤ m, so B_L=S_f(1) for f=1−w³/2, with H=0, K=a_m and trace exactly zero. Identity (35) gives U_p(L) ≡ 4B_L−(34/9)a_L and U_p(m) ≡ 4B_L+(8/9)a_m.

Theorem R (elementary): by Wilson reflection with n=2m, p−1−2m=m+1 ≡ 1/3, and 8^m=2^{p−2} ≡ 1/2, so C(2m,m) ≡ −3/(m!)³, a_m ≡ −6/(m!)³ and a_L ≡ 3/(2(m!)³). Hence U_p(L) ≡ 4B_L−17/(3(m!)³). The p ≡ 1 (mod 3) companion C(2L,L) ≡ −1/(L!)³ ≡ −r is what Jacobi's congruence evaluates (Cosgrave & Dilcher, 2018, Corollary 4.5). No such evaluation is available for p ≡ 2 (mod 3).

Theorem E: cubing is a bijection of F_p, with inverse exponent (2p−1)/3. Therefore ⌊p/3⌋! mod p, a_L, a_m, U_p(L) for p ≠ 17, U_p(m) for all p ≥ 7, and the branch value T_f(μ) at μ=2^{−m} are mutually computable with O(log p) extra field operations, given the retained fast prefix. At p=17 the coefficient 34/9 vanishes, so U_p(6)=4B_6. A polylogarithmic complete U at the rounded third point would therefore give a polylogarithmic ⌊p/3⌋! mod p. This is an implication, not a lower bound.

Proposition B: the classical identity Σ_{i≤n}C(2i,i)4^{−i}=(2n+1)C(2n,n)4^{−n} gives T_f(μ)=−a_m/(3μ) elementarily and hence (B6). It agrees with the general branch formula on this family, which is a consistency check, not a general proof. Γ_p: since m is odd and (p+1)/3 ≡ 1/3, m! ≡ Γ_p(1/3) (mod p), so K ≡ −6/Γ_p(1/3)³. Coleman (1990) and Ogus (1990) are plausible prior sources for this identification but were not read. Gross–Koblitz over F_{p²} involves only Γ_p(1/3)Γ_p(2/3)=±1; this assessment needs review.

Answer to the roadmap question: CM trace data does not suffice, since the trace is identically zero. No closed form for ⌊p/3⌋! mod p was located; Cosgrave and Dilcher (2018) call the case of moduli without prime factors ≡ 1 (mod M) "utterly intractable". The best known classical single-prime factorial method costs about √p operations (Bostan et al., 2007; Tal, 2026, unrefereed, states that no classical sub-square-root single-input algorithm is known). For many primes together, the remainder-tree technique of Costa, Gerbicz and Harvey (2014) is assessed, without verification, to give average polylogarithmic cost per prime.

Evidence: validate_rounded_third.py (standard library) checked all 1,136 primes p ≡ 2 (mod 3) with 5 ≤ p ≤ 20,000. It also checked dense polynomial powers at 57 primes up to 600 and the p ≡ 1 (mod 3) companion at 330 primes below 5,000. There were no failures, and the run took about 6 seconds. (m!)³ ≡ ±1 occurred only at p=5, 17 and 5987. No timings or benchmark are claimed.

Status: the rounded third point is not a fast complete-U family. The precise obstruction is recorded, and the branch should be parked unless a batch workload over many primes is wanted. Novelty is not claimed; the reduction is short and its ingredients are classical. The prior-art search was targeted, not exhaustive (PRIOR_ART_ASSESSMENT_20261009.md).

## 18 October 2026: rounded third point, certified Jacobi boundary backend

Question: can the rounded-third boundary a_L, equivalently G = ⌊p/3⌋! mod p, be acquired fast enough in practice that the retained O(log p) Miller prefix yields faster complete evaluation of U_p(L)? And for which workloads?

Construction: integrated.py registers a custom exact reduction with the retained BoundaryEvaluator and delegates the sum to complete_sum. B_L comes from the unchanged ThirdPointPrefixProvider. The boundary routes are:
- native FLINT factorial;
- an exact nodal factorial-ratio anchor;
- certified split Jacobi reconstruction (T3) for a prime conductor 5 ≤ ℓ ≤ 89 dividing p−1, with shortened rounded-third anchor intervals of about k/3 and k/6, or k/3 and k/12, where k = (p−1)/ℓ.

A T3 reconstruction is accepted only after its algebraic and root-of-unity normalization certificates pass. Automatic mode uses native factorial below 10^7. Above that, a frozen cost predictor decides, with nodal acquisition as the unconditional fallback. No class numbers, PARI or GRH are used.

Identities (proved, elementary): a_L = 3/(2G^3), G = (3/(2a_L))^((2p−1)/3), a_m = −4a_L, B_m = B_L + 4a_L, U_p(L) = 4B_L − (17/3)G^(−3), U_p(m) = 4B_L − (16/3)G^(−3), and U_p(L) − U_p(m) = −(1/3)G^(−3) ≠ 0. At p = 17 the multiplier 34/9 vanishes, so U_17(6) = 4B_6, and the boundary must be checked separately there.

De Rham interpretation (companion, unrefereed): on y^2 = x^3 + 1/4 with ω = dx/y, the Cartier and absolute-Frobenius matrices in the specified Čech basis are [[0, 2a_L],[0, 0]] and [[0, −2a_L],[0, 0]].

Evidence (recorded in the package; counts overlap):
- development: all 1,135 admissible primes below 20,000, with 3,405 complete method checks, 1,135 integer-binomial checks, 1,135 independent prefix comparisons and 200 forced T3 calls;
- fresh confirmation: 17 primes up to 7,000,000,009,553;
- exploratory survey: 39,264 primes below 10^6, with no zero of U_p(L) or U_p(m) observed.
