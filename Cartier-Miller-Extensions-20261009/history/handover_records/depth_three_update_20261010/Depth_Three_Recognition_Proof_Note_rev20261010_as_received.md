# Direct recognition of one depth-three family: the ordinary j=0 seed at conductor 8

7 October 2026, revised 10 October 2026 (deterministic trace sign). Separate unrefereed extension note for the Cartier–Miller evaluation project. The published manuscript is unchanged; the repository URL and commit remain unknown and GitHub has not been inspected. The note distinguishes algebraic derivations, which require human review, from executable identities checked in finite fields, and from deferred or conjectural material. No novelty or wall-time benchmark is claimed. A second, independent worker re-derived every constant, attacked the implementation and verified the literature before this version was written; its report is retained under `independent/` and its criticisms are reconciled in the penultimate section.

## Outcome in one paragraph

Starting from the retained modulo-73 target, the depth-three nondual closure of the ordinary easy seed $A=0$ is a single explicit family over the biquadratic field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$, parametrized by a nonzero twist parameter $r$ together with a choice of signs $e=\pm\sqrt3$, $g=\pm\sqrt2$, with short coefficients $A=\alpha(e,g)r^2$, $B=\gamma(e,g)r^3$ and second-kind coefficient $\beta=-(7+6e+6g+2eg)\,rH$. Its kernel roots are rational and its seed ordinary precisely when $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$. The four conjugate $j$-invariants are the roots of the classical class polynomial of discriminant $-192$. A direct recognizer extracts $e$ and $g$ from the supplied coefficients $(A,B)$ by two relative norms in $\mathbb F_p[e,g]/(e^2-3,g^2-2)$, then $r$, and verifies both coefficient equations together with the forward and reverse rooted paths; it uses a constant number of field operations, no square-root algorithm and no graph search. Exact integer factorizations show that at every eligible prime the extraction denominators are nonzero for every family member, the models are nonsingular, the conjugates are distinct, and no collision with any depth-at-most-two class or with the other depth-three chain occurs. Every quadratic twist is absorbed by $r\mapsto dr$. The exact trace of an accepted curve is not an independent unknown: it equals the trace of its $j=0$ seed and is $\pm t_0$ for the unique representation $4p=t_0^2+192f^2$, so a complete preparation needs a Cornacchia representation and one sign bit rather than point counting, and the missing coefficient $K$ of the original normalized cubic then follows in constant field work. The companion depth-three chain from the $B=0$ seed is deferred: its class field is dihedral rather than biquadratic and needs a different extraction.

## Normalized data and the transport step

Let $p\ge7$ be prime and $E:y^2=F(x)=x^3+Ax+B$ nonsingular over $\mathbb F_p$, $h=(p-1)/2$, $H=[x^{p-1}]F^h$, $\beta=[x^{p-2}]F^h$, $\omega=dx/y$, $\eta=x\omega$. The absolute Cartier operator satisfies $C(\omega)=H\omega$ and $C(\eta)=\beta\omega$ (Katz, Section 1), and $H\equiv\tau\pmod p$ for the exact trace $\tau$. The easy seed used here is $A=0$ with $p\equiv1\pmod3$: every exponent of $(x^3+B)^h$ is a multiple of three while $p-2$ is not, so $\beta=0$, and $H=\binom{h}{(p-1)/3}B^{h-(p-1)/3}$ is a nonzero binomial coefficient with arguments below $p$ times a nonzero constant, so the seed is ordinary.

For a rational root $q$ of $F$ the retained explicitly normalized quotient is
$$A'=-4A-15q^2,\qquad B'=-8Aq-22q^3,\qquad \phi^*\omega'=\omega,\qquad \phi^*\eta'=(2x-q)\,\omega-2\,d\!\left(\frac{y}{x-q}\right).$$
Because this note relies on it three times in succession, the identities were re-derived in this pass rather than imported. With $u=A+3q^2=F'(q)$ the point map $x'=x+u/(x-q)$, $y'=y\bigl(1-u/(x-q)^2\bigr)$ satisfies $y'^2=x'^3+A'x'+B'$ modulo $y^2=F(x)$; its differential is $dx'/y'=dx/y$ exactly; and since $d(y/(x-q))=\bigl(F'(x)(x-q)-2F(x)\bigr)/(2(x-q)^2)\cdot\omega$ one has $x'\omega=(2x-q)\omega-2\,d(y/(x-q))$ as rational functions. These three facts are verified as exact rational-function identities by `theory/verify_transport.py`, and the independent worker proved them again from Vélu's form of the map. The Cartier step is the standard one: the quotient is separable because $p$ is odd, so a separating variable of the target function field remains a separating variable of the source, the unique $p$-basis decomposition is preserved, and $C$ commutes with $\phi^*$; exact differentials are killed. Hence $H'=H$ and $\beta'=2\beta-qH$, with differential scale $+1$ on every edge and no local-principal-part substitution. The same script confirms that the distinguished dual root $-2q$ of the target returns $(16A,64B)$, the square scaling $d=4$, and proves the cofactor lemma used below: on the target, $x^3+A'x+B'=(x+2q)\bigl(x^2-2qx+(A'+4q^2)\bigr)$ and the quadratic cofactor has discriminant $16F'(q)$, so the two nondual roots of the target are
$$q\pm2\sqrt{F'(q)}=q\pm2\sqrt{A+3q^2}.$$
This lemma determines every nondual continuation of a rooted chain from the data of the previous edge alone.

## The family

Fix the seed $E_0:(0,-r^3)$ with $r\ne0$; every $A=0$ model with a rational two-torsion point has this form, because $x^3+B$ has a rational root exactly when $-B$ is a cube. At $p\equiv1\pmod3$ the seed has three rational roots $r,\omega r,\omega^2r$ with $\omega$ a primitive cube root of unity; the chain started at $\omega r$ is the chain of the same seed written as $(0,-(\omega r)^3)$ with parameter $\omega r$, so letting $r$ range over all of $\mathbb F_p^\times$ absorbs this choice and nothing below depends on which root is called $r$. The first kernel root is $q_0=r$, giving $E_1:(-15r^2,-22r^3)$ and $\beta_1=-rH$. The cofactor lemma with $F_0'(r)=3r^2$ gives the nondual roots $q_1=(1\pm2e)r$ with $e^2=3$; the retained depth-two family $E_2:\bigl((-135-60e)r^2,(-694-420e)r^3\bigr)$, $\beta_2=-(3+2e)rH$, follows. The new step applies the lemma once more. Here $F_1'(q_1)=A_1+3q_1^2=\bigl(-15+3(13+4e)\bigr)r^2=12(2+e)r^2$, and since $(3+e)^2\cdot2=24+12e$ one has $\sqrt{12(2+e)}=(3+e)g$ with $g^2=2$. Therefore the nondual third kernel roots are
$$q_2=\bigl(1+2e\pm2(3+e)g\bigr)r=(1+2e+6g+2eg)\,r,$$
the sign being absorbed into the choice of $g$. Substituting into the quotient gives the family
$$\boxed{A=\alpha(e,g)\,r^2,\quad B=\gamma(e,g)\,r^3,\qquad
\alpha=-1095-540e-540g-420eg,\quad
\gamma=-22198-13860e-16380g-8820eg,}$$
and the transport $\beta_3=2\beta_2-q_2H$ gives
$$\boxed{\beta=-(7+6e+6g+2eg)\,rH.}$$
The coordinates were obtained by hand, by a sympy script in $\mathbb Z[e,g]/(e^2-3,g^2-2)$, by a standard-library integer script that re-runs the three quotients from the seed, and by the independent worker's own ring arithmetic; all agree. The chain is nondual at each step: $q_1=-2q_0$ would force $3=0$, and $q_2=-2q_1$ would force $3+6e+6g+2eg=0$, whose norm to $\mathbb Q$ is $9$. The two nondual third roots are distinct because $(3+e)g$ has norm $144$.

The reverse certificate is the dual chain. With $q_i$ the forward roots the inverse normalized roots are $-2\cdot4^iq_{2-i}$, that is
$$(-2q_2,\,-8q_1,\,-32q_0)=\bigl(-(2+4e+12g+4eg)r,\;-(8+16e)r,\;-32r\bigr),$$
and the endpoint is the seed scaled by $d=4^3$, namely $(0,-(64r)^3)$, which is again an $A=0$ seed. The reverse multiplier $\sum_i q^{\rm rev}_i/2^{i+1}=-q_2-2q_1-4q_0$ equals $-(7+6e+6g+2eg)r$ without any division by $H$, in agreement with the forward transport. At $p=73$ with $(e,g,r)=(21,41,42)$ this reproduces the retained fixture exactly: forward roots $(42,54,1)$, models $(0,7)\to(39,8)\to(50,22)\to(4,16)$, reverse roots $(71,6,43)$, endpoint $(0,7)$ and multiplier $15$, so that $\beta=15\cdot10=4$.

## Field of definition, ordinary primes and completeness of the closure

The kernel roots lie in $\mathbb F_p$ exactly when $e$ and $g$ do, which by the quadratic characters of $3$ and $2$ means $p\equiv\pm1\pmod{12}$ and $p\equiv\pm1\pmod8$. The seed is ordinary when $p\equiv1\pmod3$. Together these force $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$, and conversely every such prime admits the full construction with all four sign choices of $(e,g)$ and every nonzero $r$. The coefficients $\alpha,\gamma$ are also rational at $p\equiv23\pmod{24}$, where both square roots exist, but there the seed is supersingular and the construction is outside the ordinary scope; the eligibility condition is the conjunction of rationality and ordinarity. The smallest eligible prime is $73$, which is why the retained audit found its first depth-three $A=0$-seed classes there. At $p\equiv13\pmod{24}$ the depth-two family exists but, since $12(2+e)=2(3+e)^2$ is then a nonsquare, no rational nondual third step exists at all; this is an exact obstruction, not a search miss. In fact more is true and consistent with CM theory: at such primes $H_{-192}$ has no root modulo $p$ at all, because a root would give an ordinary curve whose Frobenius has norm $p$ in the order of discriminant $-192$, forcing $p=x^2+48y^2\equiv1\pmod8$; the independent worker verified the absence of roots at $13,37,61,109,157,181$. This remark is not used below.

The construction is also exhaustive. Any rooted chain of length three from an $A=0$ seed with no immediate dual step must take the displayed form: the seed must be $(0,-r^3)$ for one of its three roots $r$, the first quotient is then determined, and the cofactor lemma leaves only the displayed nondual roots at the second and third steps. Hence for $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$ the set of endpoints of nondual rational degree-two chains of length three from $A=0$ seeds is exactly
$$\mathcal F_3(p)=\{(\alpha(e,g)r^2,\gamma(e,g)r^3):\ e^2=3,\ g^2=2,\ r\in\mathbb F_p^\times\},$$
which has $4(p-1)$ distinct models, while for $p\equiv13\pmod{24}$ this set is empty. The parameters are injective on $\mathcal F_3(p)$ at eligible primes by the uniqueness statement of the recognition theorem below. The validation suite compares $\mathcal F_3(p)$ with an independent brute-force enumeration of all nondual length-three chains from every $(0,B)$ at ten eligible primes and finds equality in each case.

The minimal polynomial of $j=6912\alpha^3/(4\alpha^3+27\gamma^2)$ over $\mathbb Q$, obtained by multiplying the four conjugate linear factors in $\mathbb Z[e,g]$ and dividing by the norm of the discriminant, is
$$H_{-192}(J)=J^4-8041801037378436000\,J^3+15705521635909735050750000\,J^2+826335556188178615474500000000\,J-1080060886113159937649308593750000.$$
PARI's `polclass(-192)` returns the same polynomial, its Galois group is the Klein four-group, and Lario's author-curated table lists it for $D=-192$ with $\mathbb Q(j)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$. This is the expected position on the $2$-isogeny volcano descending from $j=0$: the seed has conductor $1$, and depths one, two and three have conductors $2$, $4$ and $8$ in $\mathbb Z[\omega]$, discriminants $-12$, $-48$ and $-192$. The identification is a verified consistency check and the curves and polynomial are classical; it is used below only through Kohel's structure theorem, in the trace section.

## Direct recognition

Let $R=\mathbb F_p[e,g]/(e^2-3,g^2-2)$ with basis $(1,e,g,eg)$. When $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$ and $(e_0,g_0)$ are fixed square roots of $3$ and $2$, the four ring homomorphisms $\chi_{s,t}:R\to\mathbb F_p$, $\chi_{s,t}(e,g)=(se_0,tg_0)$, identify $R$ with $\mathbb F_p^4$. Reduce the integer powers
$$\alpha^3=a_0+a_1e+a_2g+a_3eg,\qquad \gamma^2=c_0+c_1e+c_2g+c_3eg,$$
whose exact coordinates are in the table below. Given short coefficients $(A,B)$ with $AB\ne0$, form
$$L=B^2\alpha^3-A^3\gamma^2=U_0+U_1e+U_2g+U_3eg,\qquad U_i=B^2a_i-A^3c_i,$$
and the two relative norms
$$L\,\sigma_g(L)=P_0+P_1e,\qquad P_0=U_0^2+3U_1^2-2U_2^2-6U_3^2,\quad P_1=2(U_0U_1-2U_2U_3),$$
$$L\,\sigma_e(L)=Q_0+Q_1g,\qquad Q_0=U_0^2-3U_1^2+2U_2^2-6U_3^2,\quad Q_1=2(U_0U_2-3U_1U_3),$$
where $\sigma_e$ and $\sigma_g$ negate $e$ and $g$ respectively. The recognizer requires $P_1Q_1\ne0$ and sets
$$\boxed{e=-P_0/P_1,\qquad g=-Q_0/Q_1,\qquad r=\frac{\alpha(e,g)\,B}{\gamma(e,g)\,A},}$$
then checks $e^2=3$, $g^2=2$, $r\ne0$, $A=\alpha(e,g)r^2$, $B=\gamma(e,g)r^3$, and finally re-executes the forward chain from $(0,-r^3)$ and the reverse chain to $(0,-(64r)^3)$ with the retained path checker. It never computes a square root, never searches the isogeny graph, and never divides by $H$.

**Theorem (recognition).** Let $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$ and let $(A,B)$ be a nonsingular short model. If the recognizer accepts, then $(A,B)\in\mathcal F_3(p)$ with the exhibited parameters and $\beta(A,B)=-(7+6e+6g+2eg)\,rH$. Conversely every $(A,B)\in\mathcal F_3(p)$ is accepted, its parameters $(e,g,r)$ are uniquely determined by $(A,B)$, and $AB\ne0$.

Soundness is immediate: acceptance means the parameter equations hold, so $(A,B)$ is literally the endpoint of the displayed chain, which the certificate re-executes, and the coefficient identity is the transport statement of the previous section. For completeness let $(A,B)=(\alpha_0r_0^2,\gamma_0r_0^3)$ with $\alpha_0=\chi_0(\alpha)$, $\gamma_0=\chi_0(\gamma)$ for one character $\chi_0$. Then $L=r_0^6(\gamma_0^2\alpha^3-\alpha_0^3\gamma^2)$ as an element of $R$, so $\chi_0(L)=0$. For an automorphism $\sigma$ put $C_\sigma=\gamma^2\sigma(\alpha)^3-\alpha^3\sigma(\gamma)^2\in\mathbb Z[e,g]$; then $\chi_0\sigma(L)=r_0^6\chi_0(C_\sigma)$. Applying $\chi_0$ to $L\sigma_g(L)=P_0+P_1e$ gives $P_0+P_1e_0=\chi_0(L)\chi_0\sigma_g(L)=0$, and applying $\chi_0\sigma_e$ gives $P_0-P_1e_0=r_0^{12}\chi_0(C_{\sigma_e})\chi_0(C_{\sigma_e\sigma_g})$. Hence
$$P_1=-\frac{r_0^{12}\,\chi_0(C_{\sigma_e})\,\chi_0(C_{\sigma_e\sigma_g})}{2e_0},\qquad
Q_1=-\frac{r_0^{12}\,\chi_0(C_{\sigma_g})\,\chi_0(C_{\sigma_e\sigma_g})}{2g_0},$$
the second by the same argument with the roles of $e$ and $g$ exchanged. The integer norm $N(C_\sigma)$ reduces modulo $p$ to the product of the four values $\chi(C_\sigma)$, so if $p\nmid N(C_\sigma)$ then every $\chi_0(C_\sigma)$ is nonzero. The factorizations below show that no prime $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$ divides any of the three norms; therefore $P_1Q_1\ne0$, $e=-P_0/P_1=e_0$ and $g=-Q_0/Q_1=g_0$. Since no eligible prime divides $N(\alpha)N(\gamma)$, $\alpha_0\gamma_0\ne0$, so $r=\alpha_0B/(\gamma_0A)=r_0$ and both coefficient equations hold. Uniqueness follows because $e$, $g$ and $r$ are given by these explicit formulas in $(A,B)$. The vanishing of $\chi_0(C_\sigma)$ is exactly the coincidence $j_{\chi_0}=j_{\chi_0\sigma}$ in $\mathbb F_p$, because $j-\sigma(j)=-6912\cdot27\,C_\sigma/(\Delta\,\sigma(\Delta))$ with $\Delta=4\alpha^3+27\gamma^2$; so the theorem can be read as saying that at eligible primes the four conjugate $j$-values are distinct and the input's $j$ selects its conjugate, after which $r$ restores the coordinate factor that a $j$-only test would lose. The equivalent linear-algebra formulation is that the row vector $(1,e_0,g_0,e_0g_0)$ spans the kernel of the $4\times4$ multiplication-by-$L$ matrix; the relative norms are its closed form. For inputs outside the family the denominators $P_1$ or $Q_1$ can vanish; the independent worker showed these inputs are exactly the twists of two $j$-values per prime, roots of fixed integer quadratics in $B^2/A^3$, and all of them are correctly rejected.

Here $P_2$ and $P_3$ are the retained depth-two class polynomials of discriminants $-64$ and $-48$; $0$, $1728$, $54000$, $287496$ are the $j$-values of the seeds and the one-step families; and $H_{-256}$ is PARI's `polclass(-256)`, the class polynomial of the depth-three chain below $j=1728$. No prime factor is congruent to $1$ modulo $24$. Consequently, at every eligible prime, $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ are nonzero for all four conjugates, every family model is nonsingular, the four conjugate $j$-values are pairwise distinct, the extraction denominators $P_1$ and $Q_1$ are nonzero for every family member, and no family $j$-value equals any $j$-value of the complete depth-at-most-two closure or of the $B=0$ depth-three chain. Since at eligible primes that closure consists of every curve whose $j$ lies in its $j$-set, including all twists, and since $\mathcal F_3(p)$ likewise contains both quadratic twists of each of its $j$-values, the family is disjoint from the depth-at-most-two closure as a set of models, not merely as a set of $j$-values. These are global factorization statements, not extrapolations from the finite census. They are also what classical CM theory predicts: an ordinary curve with $j$ a root of $H_{-192}$ has endomorphism ring of discriminant $-192$ by Deuring's reduction theorem, so it cannot share a $j$-value with a curve of discriminant $-3$, $-12$, $-48$, $-4$, $-16$, $-64$ or $-256$, and the four conjugates are distinct at primes splitting completely in the ring class field. The residue pattern in the table is the same statement seen prime by prime: $j_\chi=j_{\chi\sigma_e}$ happens only at $p\equiv17\pmod{24}$, where $\sqrt2$ is rational and $\sqrt3$ is not, and similarly $11$ for $\sigma_g$ and $5$ for the composite. The factorizations are kept as the self-contained proof because they do not depend on any theorem that was not reopened in a primary source.

Degenerations are real outside the eligible class and are preserved rather than hidden. At $p\in\{23,47,71,167,191\}$, all congruent to $23$ modulo $24$, both square roots exist but the seed is supersingular; at $47,71,167,191$ exactly one of the four sign choices has $\gamma\equiv0$, collapsing that conjugate to $j=1728$, while at $p=23$ two sign choices have $\gamma\equiv0$ and a third has $\alpha\equiv0$, collapsing to $j=0$, as the exponents $23^2$ in $N(\gamma)$ and $23^1$ in $N(\alpha)$ predict. The recognizer reports `not_eligible_prime` for every input at such primes instead of applying the ordinary reconstruction. At the collision primes $29,41,53,59,83,89,101,113,131,149,173,179$ at least one of $\sqrt2,\sqrt3$ is irrational, so no base-field parameter model exists at all, and the coincidence is between non-rational conjugates. The validation suite records, for every tested excluded prime, the number of rational-parameter models and how many have $\alpha$, $\gamma$ or the discriminant equal to zero.

A displayed quadratic twist $d\ne0$ sends $(A,B)$ to $(d^2A,d^3B)$, $H$ to $\chi(d)H$ and $\beta$ to $d\chi(d)\beta$ by the retained polynomial identity $F_d(x)=d^3F(x/d)$. In the family this is exactly $r\mapsto dr$ with $(e,g)$ unchanged, and the multiplier becomes $d\cdot(-(7+6e+6g+2eg)r)$, so $\beta_d=d\,\chi(d)\,\beta$ is reproduced without computing $\chi(d)$: the certificate multiplies by the final model's own $H$. The square scaling $d=s^2$ is the base-field isomorphism $x\mapsto s^2x$, $y\mapsto s^3y$, whose differential scale is $1/s$; the certificate never replaces a model by an isomorphic one and keeps scale $+1$ on each edge.

## The trace of an accepted curve and complete preparation

The earlier notes charged $T_{\rm Schoof}$ for the trace of every accepted curve. For this family that overstates the cost, because the trace is determined by $p$ up to sign.

**Proposition (trace).** Let $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$ and $(A,B)\in\mathcal F_3(p)$ with parameter $r$. Then the exact trace $\tau$ of $(A,B)$ equals the exact trace of the seed $(0,-r^3)$, and $\tau=\pm t_0$ where $(t_0,f)$ is the unique pair of positive integers with $4p=t_0^2+192f^2$.

The equality of traces is already proved: $H$ is transported unchanged along the three edges, so $\tau\equiv\tau_{\rm seed}\pmod p$, and both lie in the Hasse interval of width $4\sqrt p<p$. For the seed, the map $(x,y)\mapsto(\omega x,y)$ with $\omega$ a primitive cube root of unity in $\mathbb F_p$ is an automorphism, so $\mathbb Z[\omega]\subseteq\operatorname{End}(E_0)$; since $E_0$ is ordinary its endomorphism ring is an order in an imaginary quadratic field containing the maximal order $\mathbb Z[\omega]$, hence equal to it, and the Frobenius $\pi_0\in\mathbb Z[\omega]$ has norm $p$ and trace $\tau$, so $4p=\tau^2+3s^2$ for an integer $s$. The refinement $8\mid s$ comes from the $2$-isogeny volcano of the component containing $j=0$. Because $2$ is inert in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$, the surface of that volcano is the single vertex $j=0$ with no horizontal edges, every vertex strictly between the surface and the floor has exactly one edge up and two down, and the edge up is the dual of the edge by which the vertex was reached; a path with no immediate dual step therefore descends one level per edge, so the family lies at level three and the volcano has depth $d\ge3$. Kohel's structure theorem, in the form stated by Sutherland (*Isogeny volcanoes*, Theorem 7(iv)), gives $d=\nu_2\bigl((\tau^2-4p)/D_0\bigr)/2$ with $D_0=-3$, that is $d=\nu_2(s)$, so $8\mid s$ and $4p=\tau^2+192f^2$ with $s=8f$. Theorem 7 as printed excludes components containing $0$ or $1728$, and Sutherland's Remark 8 states that the only modification for the component of $0$ is the three-to-one collapse of the surface vertex's edges, which is exactly the single-$j$ depth-one layer observed here; the theorem is applied with that caveat recorded. Finally, uniqueness: two distinct representations $4p=t^2+3s^2$ with $8\mid s$ would be related by a unit of $\mathbb Z[\omega]$ other than $\pm1$, and if $\pi$ and $\omega\pi$ both had $8\mid s$ then $\tau\equiv s\pmod{16}$ would force $8\mid\tau$ and $16\mid4p$, which is absurd. The independent worker verified the proposition at six primes and the validation suite verifies it, together with the uniqueness of the representation by brute force, at all $46$ eligible primes below $3000$.

The proposition turns trace preparation into two cheap steps. A square root of $-3$ is $2z+1$ for any primitive cube root of unity $z=c^{(p-1)/3}$ with $c$ a cubic nonresidue, and Cornacchia's algorithm applied to $p=x^2+48y^2$ with the square root $4(2z+1)$ of $-48$ returns $t_0=2x$ and $f=y$. The only remaining datum is the sign of $\tau$, and since an accepted curve has the trace of its seed $y^2=x^3+D$ with $D=-r^3$, the sign follows from the classical evaluation of that seed by Jacobi sums. Ireland and Rosen (1990, Ch. 18, Section 3, Theorem 4) state: for $p\equiv1\pmod3$, $p\nmid D$, write $p=\pi\bar\pi$ with $\pi\in\mathbb Z[\omega]$ and $\pi\equiv2\pmod3$; then $N_p=p+1+\overline{(4D/\pi)_6}\,\pi+(4D/\pi)_6\,\bar\pi$, so $\tau=-2\,\mathrm{Re}\bigl(\overline{(4D/\pi)_6}\,\pi\bigr)$. The primary $\pi$ is read off the Cornacchia output: with $\sqrt{-3}=1+2\omega$, the element $x+4y\sqrt{-3}=(x+4y)+8y\,\omega$ has norm $p$, and exactly one of its six unit multiples $a+b\omega$ has $a\equiv2$, $b\equiv0\pmod3$. Modulo $\pi$ one has $\omega\equiv-a/b$, so the sextic symbol is the power of the image of $1+\omega$ equal to $(4D)^{(p-1)/6}\bmod p$. This costs one exponentiation, is deterministic, and the code checks $|\tau|=t_0$ as a consistency guard. The prototype reproduces the book's worked example ($p=13$, $D=1$, $\pi=-1+3\omega$, $N_{13}=12$) and agrees with Legendre-symbol traces for all $6{,}900$ curves $y^2=x^3+D$ at the $37$ primes $p\equiv1\pmod3$ below $400$, and with the family's traces at all $46$ eligible primes below $3000$. The only randomized step left in the complete preparation is the cubic-nonresidue search that produces $\sqrt{-3}$ for Cornacchia; replacing it deterministically is a separate classical problem that is not addressed here. The earlier Las Vegas point test (for a random rational point exactly one of $[p+1\mp t_0]P$ vanishes unless the trial is inconclusive) is retained as `sign_method='las_vegas'` and agrees with the sextic rule in every validation case; no bound on its trial count is claimed. Schoof's algorithm remains the unconditional deterministic fallback. The same proposition gives a necessary condition that a caller-supplied trace must satisfy, $4p-\tau^2=192f^2$ with $f\ge1$, which the prototype now enforces; it rejects the impossible values $11$ and $0$ at $p=73$ that the Hasse interval alone admits, and with `verify_sign=True` it also rejects the wrong-sign value $-10$ by the sextic rule.

## Reconstruction of K on the original cubic and costs

For the retained cubic $f(w)=1+cw+bw^2+aw^3$, $a\ne0$, the fixed normalization $x=aw+b/3$, $y=av$ has $dw/v=dx/y$, short model $A=ac-b^2/3$, $B=a^2-abc/3+2b^3/27$, and $\beta=aK+(b/3)H$, hence $K=(\beta-(b/3)H)/a$. Equivalently the point $P_+=(0,1)$ of $v^2=f(w)$ maps to the rational point $(x_0,y_0)=(b/3,a)$ of $E$, and every normalized cubic with short model $(A,B)$ arises from a rational point with $y_0\ne0$ as $f=1+\bigl(F'(x_0)/y_0\bigr)w+3x_0w^2+y_0w^3$; its rational branch roots are $\mu=(q-x_0)/y_0$ for the two-torsion abscissae $q$. Once the recognizer accepts the short model and the exact trace is in hand, $H$ is its reduction, $\beta$ is the certified multiplier times $H$, and $K$ follows; the retained branch formula $T_f(\mu)=a\mu(\mu H-K)/f'(\mu)$ is then applied unchanged. The $\beta$ used must be that of the cubic's own short model: for the fixture cubic $f=1+4w+37w^3$ at $p=73$ the short model is $(2,55)$, the $d=16$ twist of $(4,16)$, the recognizer returns $r=16\cdot42$ and multiplier $21$, and the trace $10$, whether supplied or obtained from $73=5^2+48\cdot1^2$ and one sign trial, gives $H=10$, $\beta=64$, $K=55$ and $T_f(9)=3$, agreeing with the retained dense expansion.

| Task | Cost and status |
|---|---|

| Sign of the trace, alternative | Las Vegas point test: per trial one random point and two scalar multiplications of $O(\log p)$ field operations; trial count reported, no proved bound |

Practical SEA or PARI `ellap` are used in this pass only as independent checks, never as part of the proof or the delivered preparation. A constant-time cached query is not a complete run, there are at most three rational branches per cubic, and nothing here supplies $B_L$ and $a_L$ for an original weighted sum, a uniform stopping-index result, or a sum-avoiding Witt correction.

## What is proved, what is tested, what is conjectured, what is deferred

The algebraic content requiring human review consists of the cofactor lemma, the three-step derivation of $\alpha$, $\gamma$ and the multiplier, the relative-norm extraction with its completeness argument, the twist absorption, the integer-factorization exclusions, and the trace proposition, whose only external dependency is Kohel's volcano theorem as stated by Sutherland with the Remark 8 caveat. Each identity among these was also executed exactly in $\mathbb Z[e,g]$ or in $\mathbb F_p$, so an error could only lie in the framing of the Cartier step or in the identification of what the quotient formula represents, both of which are inherited from the retained notes and were re-checked here and by the independent worker.

The finite evidence consists of the following, all passing, with counts that overlap and must not be added. At the ten primes $73,97,193,241,313,337,409,433,457,577$ every one of the $1{,}215{,}024$ nonsingular short models was classified, the $12{,}480$ accepted models coincide exactly with the independent parametrized enumeration and with the brute-force set of nondual depth-three endpoints from $A=0$ seeds, and the recovered parameters agree with the construction. For $2{,}640$ family members the exact $(H,\beta)$ were recomputed by integer multinomial expansion, $H$ was matched against Legendre-symbol traces and PARI `ellap`, $H\ne0$ was confirmed and $\beta$ equalled the certified multiplier times $H$; this covers every member at the four smallest primes. Twists were checked in $702$ cases including every $d$ at $p=73$. Fifteen tampered certificates were rejected, including ones that alter only the stored reverse path, model list, seed or depth, since the verifier now compares every stored field with the recomputed certificate; four impossible or wrong-sign supplied traces and six invalid inputs were rejected. The $96$ retained audit certificates behave as predicted: the $32$ depth-three rows from $A=0$ seeds are recognized with the audit's roots and multipliers, the $32$ depth-three rows from $B=0$ seeds are rejected by this family, and the $32$ depth-four rows are rejected. Forty-eight original normalized cubics built from random rational points at five primes gave $K$ equal to the retained $O(p)$ recurrence and $96$ branch queries equal to dense summation. Family members were recognized at random primes of $17$, $21$, $32$, $41$ and $63$ bits; at the two smallest of these the $O(p)$ recurrence independently confirmed $K$, while at the three largest only the certificate, the reverse path and the PARI trace were checked, so the value of $K$ there rests on the proof. The trace proposition and the uniqueness of the representation were verified at all $46$ eligible primes below $3000$, and $27$ complete CM preparations, including primes of $32$, $41$ and $63$ bits, agreed with Legendre or PARI traces and, where available, with the $O(p)$ recurrence for $K$. The retained $p=13$ obstruction $(1,1)$ is outside the eligible class and is unaffected.

Conjectural or deferred material is confined to the $B=0$ seed. The audit's depth-three classes from $j=1728$ seeds at $p=73,89,113,233$ belong to a second chain whose third kernel roots exist when $4+3e$ is a square for $e^2=2$, by the same cofactor lemma applied to $F_1'(s)=4(4+3e)r^2$; this condition is exact. Descending three levels below $j=1728$ puts that chain at conductor $8$ in $\mathbb Z[i]$, discriminant $-256$, which is why its $j$-values at $p=73$ are the roots of `polclass(-256)` (checked by the independent worker) and why this recognizer rejects it: the resultant row above excludes any $j$-collision at eligible primes, and the two chains have different CM fields. The polynomial $H_{-256}$ has dihedral Galois group and cyclic class group, so $\mathbb Q(j)$ is a non-Galois quartic field and the biquadratic character algebra used here is unavailable. The finite observation that the primes $73,89,113,233$ are of the form $x^2+64y^2$ while $97,193,241$ are not is the expected splitting condition for the ring class field of discriminant $-256$ (a classical statement, Cox's Theorem 9.2 with $n=64$ being the natural pointer), but that source was not reopened in this pass and no recognizer was attempted. Classifying all depth-three families, depth four, and generic or supersingular $K$ recovery remain outside this bounded target.

No approach was abandoned as a failure in this pass. One guard is triggered by design: the recognizer's relative-norm denominators can vanish for inputs outside the family, which is simply a rejection, and the proof shows they cannot vanish for a member at an eligible prime.

## Independent validation and reconciliation

The second worker, given only the claims and the file paths, re-derived the family in its own ring arithmetic, eliminated $j$ to the same quartic, recomputed all integer invariants, proved the transport identity from Vélu's map, enumerated the family at six primes, recomputed $(H,\beta)$ for $1{,}856$ members by multinomial expansion, and ran about seventy thousand adversarial inputs against the implementation, including composite moduli congruent to $1$ modulo $24$, same-trace non-members, denominator-vanishing inputs, fourteen certificate tamperings and the audit rows. Every number agreed and no wrong acceptance or rejection was produced. Its report also opened Lario's table, Sutherland's lecture and Katz's paper and confirmed the quotations used here, resolved Katz's bibliographic record, and searched for prior statements of the transport rule and of a discriminant $-192$ recognizer with twist recovery without finding either; that search is not a priority claim.

Its criticisms were accepted and acted on as follows. The cost framing has been replaced by the trace proposition and the CM route above, and the supplied-trace API now enforces the necessary condition and offers sign verification. The certificate verifier now compares every stored field. The role of Deuring's theorem and Kohel's structure theorem has been stated explicitly, and the rejection of the $B=0$ chain is now proved by the additional resultant rather than asserted. The description of degenerations at $p=23$ has been corrected. The phrases "two-parameter family", "two independent scripts" and "defined and ordinary precisely when" have been reworded. The stronger statement at $p\equiv13\pmod{24}$ and the relabeling $r\mapsto\omega r$ have been added. Boolean coefficients are rejected and the unreachable degeneration branches raise a distinct `ProofViolation`. The independent worker's remaining suggestion, a deterministic sign rule, was adopted on 10 October 2026 once its source was verified: Ireland and Rosen's Theorem 4 was read in the user-supplied copy of Chapter 18 and is now the default sign method, with the Las Vegas test kept as a cross-check.
